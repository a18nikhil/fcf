Q1. Explain the Discovery of Electronic Evidence in Computer Forensics.

Introduction

Discovery of electronic evidence means finding and identifying digital information that can help in a cybercrime investigation.

Electronic evidence includes files, emails, messages, browsing history, system logs, and deleted data.

1. Sources of Electronic Evidence

Electronic evidence can be found in different places:

  • Computers and Laptops: Files, documents, browsing history, and system logs.
  • Mobile Phones: Messages, call records, photos, and app data.
  • Storage Devices: Hard disks, USB drives, and memory cards.
  • Networks: IP addresses, network traffic, and connection records.
  • Cloud Services: Emails, online files, backups, and account activity.

2. Steps in Discovery of Electronic Evidence

1. Identify Evidence Sources

Find computers, mobile phones, storage devices, and online accounts that may contain evidence.

2. Secure the Evidence

Protect devices and data from being accessed, deleted, or changed without permission.

3. Preserve the Evidence

Make forensic copies of the data to protect the original evidence. Compare hash values to check that the copies match the original data.

4. Search for Relevant Data

Examine forensic copies to find files, emails, messages, browsing history, and other information related to the crime.

5. Recover Deleted or Hidden Data

Use forensic tools to find hidden files and recover deleted data if it is still available.

6. Document the Findings

Record where the evidence was found, how it was collected, and who handled it. Maintain the chain of custody.

3. Tools Used for Evidence Discovery

  • Autopsy: Helps examine files and recover deleted data.
  • FTK (Forensic Toolkit): Helps search and examine digital evidence.
  • Wireshark: Helps examine network traffic.
  • Volatility: Helps examine computer memory.

4. Example

Suppose someone steals confidential files from a company.

An investigator examines a forensic copy of a computer's storage and finds file access records, USB connection history, and deleted files.

This evidence may help find out how and when the files were stolen.

Conclusion

Discovery of electronic evidence helps investigators find important digital information related to a cybercrime. Proper collection and preservation make the evidence useful for further investigation and legal proceedings.
