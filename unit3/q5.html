Q5. Explain the Challenges Faced by Government Agencies in Prosecuting Organized Crime and Terrorism Using Digital Evidence.

Introduction

Organized crime means illegal activities done by a group of criminals.

Terrorism means using violence or threats to create fear and achieve certain goals.

Government agencies use digital evidence like emails, messages, call records, and online activities to investigate these crimes.

However, they face many difficulties while collecting evidence and using it in court.

1. Challenges Faced by Government Agencies

1. Encryption and Password Protection

Criminals use encryption and passwords to protect their information.

This makes it difficult for investigators to read important evidence.

2. Deleted or Hidden Evidence

Criminals may delete files, messages, and other information to hide their crimes.

Finding and recovering this evidence can be difficult.

3. Crimes Involving Different Countries

Criminals may work from different countries and store their data on foreign servers.

Different laws make it difficult to collect evidence and take legal action.

4. Maintaining Chain of Custody

Investigators must keep a record of who collected, handled, transferred, and stored the evidence.

If these records are not kept properly, the evidence may be questioned in court.

5. Proving Evidence Is Real

Investigators must prove that digital evidence is genuine and has not been changed.

Hash values help check for changes, while forensic examination helps check where the evidence came from.

6. Identifying Criminals

Criminals may use fake accounts, VPNs, and false names to hide their identity.

This makes it difficult for investigators to find the real criminals.

7. Legal and Privacy Issues

Investigators must follow legal rules while collecting digital evidence.

Evidence collected illegally may be rejected by the court.

8. Lack of Tools and Experts

Some government agencies may not have enough trained experts or modern forensic tools.

This can make investigations slower and more difficult.

2. Example

Suppose a terrorist group uses encrypted messaging apps to plan an attack.

Investigators may find online records, but encryption, fake accounts, and foreign servers make it difficult to identify the criminals and collect evidence.

They must also prove that the evidence is real and legally collected before using it in court.
