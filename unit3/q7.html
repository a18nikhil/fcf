Q7. Apply Legal and Procedural Knowledge to Address Future Issues in Computer Forensics, Such as Cloud-Based Evidence.

Introduction

Cloud-based evidence means digital information stored online, such as files, emails, photos, and other data.

Collecting cloud evidence can be difficult because the data may be stored in other countries.

Investigators must follow legal rules and proper steps to collect and protect this evidence.

1. Legal Methods to Handle Cloud-Based Evidence

1. Follow Cybercrime Laws

Investigators must follow the laws of the countries involved when collecting digital evidence.

2. Get Legal Permission

Investigators must get a search warrant or other legal permission when required to collect cloud evidence.

3. International Cooperation

Cloud data may be stored in another country.

Investigators may ask foreign authorities for help through Mutual Legal Assistance Treaties (MLATs) or other legal methods.

4. Protect Data Privacy

Investigators must follow privacy laws and collect only the information needed for the investigation.

2. Procedural Methods to Handle Cloud-Based Evidence

1. Find Cloud Evidence

Find important evidence stored in online services like Google Drive, email accounts, and other cloud services.

2. Save Evidence

Ask the cloud service provider to keep important data safe before it is deleted or changed.

3. Collect Evidence Carefully

Use proper forensic tools and methods to collect data without making unnecessary changes.

4. Check Evidence

Use hash values, when possible, to check that the collected evidence has not been changed.

Also check account details and activity logs to help confirm where the evidence came from.

5. Maintain Chain of Custody

Keep a record of who collected, handled, transferred, and stored the evidence.

This helps show that the evidence was handled properly.

3. Example

Suppose a hacker stores stolen information in Google Drive.

Investigators get the required legal permission and ask the cloud service provider to preserve and provide the data.

They collect and protect the evidence, check that it has not changed, and keep a record of who handled it so it can be used in court.
