Q4. Design a Development Plan for Searching and Seizing Computer-Related Evidence.

Introduction

Searching and seizing computer-related evidence means finding, collecting, and protecting digital evidence during a cybercrime investigation.

A proper plan helps police collect evidence safely and legally.

Development Plan for Searching and Seizing Computer-Related Evidence

1. Prepare an Investigation Plan

Identify the crime and the devices that may contain evidence.

Prepare the tools needed for the investigation.

2. Get Legal Permission

Police must get a search warrant or other legal permission when required.

This ensures that the search follows the law.

3. Secure the Crime Scene

Do not allow unauthorized people to enter or touch the devices.

This prevents evidence from being changed or deleted.

4. Find Digital Evidence

Find devices that may contain evidence, such as computers, laptops, mobile phones, hard disks, and USB drives.

5. Record Evidence Details

Take photos and note where each device was found.

Also record whether the device was switched on or off.

6. Collect and Seize Evidence

Carefully collect the devices and storage media.

Handle devices properly to avoid losing important data.

7. Protect Digital Evidence

Make forensic copies of storage devices when needed.

Use hash values to check that the copies match the original evidence.

8. Maintain Chain of Custody

Keep a record of who collected, handled, transferred, and stored the evidence.

This helps show that the evidence was handled properly.

9. Pack and Store Evidence

Label and pack each device carefully to prevent damage.

Keep the evidence in a safe place where unauthorized people cannot access it.

Example

Suppose police investigate an online banking fraud.

They get legal permission, secure the crime scene, and find the suspect's laptop and mobile phone.

They record the device details, collect the evidence carefully, make forensic copies when needed, and store everything safely while maintaining the chain of custody.
