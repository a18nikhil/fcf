Q5. Chain of Custody

Introduction

Chain of Custody (CoC) is a record that shows how digital evidence is collected, stored, used, and passed from one person to another during an investigation.

It helps show that the evidence has been handled properly and protected from unauthorized changes.

1. Collection of Evidence

Investigators collect digital evidence from computers, mobile phones, hard disks, and other devices.

They collect it carefully to avoid changing or damaging the evidence.

2. Recording Evidence Details

Investigators write down important information about the evidence.

  • What: What evidence was collected?
  • Who: Who collected the evidence?
  • Where: Where was the evidence found?
  • When: When was the evidence collected?
  • Why: Why was the evidence collected?

3. Creating a Forensic Copy

Investigators make an exact copy of the digital evidence, called a forensic image.

They check this copy instead of using the original evidence.

This helps keep the original evidence safe.

4. Checking Evidence Using Hash Values

A hash value is a digital code calculated from data to help check whether the data has changed.

Investigators compare the hash values of the original evidence and its copy.

If both hash values match, it helps show that the copy is the same as the original.

5. Safe Storage of Evidence

Investigators keep digital evidence in a safe and secure place.

Only authorized people are allowed to access it.

This protects the evidence from theft, damage, or changes.

6. Recording Every Transfer

Whenever evidence is given from one person to another, investigators record the details.

They write down who gave it, who received it, when it was transferred, and why.

This helps keep a complete record of everyone who handled the evidence.

Conclusion

Chain of Custody helps investigators keep digital evidence safe and maintain a proper record of how it was handled.

It makes the evidence more reliable and useful in court.
