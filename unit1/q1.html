Q1. Types of Cyber Crimes

Introduction

Cyber crime means doing illegal activities using computers, mobile phones, or the internet.

These crimes include stealing money, hacking accounts, cheating people online, spreading viruses, and troubling others through the internet.

Types of Cyber Crimes

1. Click Fraud

Click fraud means clicking on online advertisements again and again to earn money illegally or increase advertising costs.

Criminals may use computer programs to make fake clicks.

Example: A person uses a program to click on advertisements hundreds of times to earn money illegally.

2. Copyright Violation

Copyright violation means copying or using someone else’s copyrighted work without permission or legal rights.

It includes movies, songs, books, photographs, and computer programs.

Example: Uploading someone else’s copyrighted movie on a website without permission.

3. Cracking

Cracking means breaking passwords or computer security to enter someone’s account or system without permission.

Criminals may do this to steal information or misuse the system.

Example: Breaking someone’s email password to read their private messages.

4. Cyber Terrorism

Cyber terrorism means using computers or the internet to create fear among people for terrorist or political purposes.

Criminals may attack government websites or important services.

Example: A terrorist group attacks an electricity system to stop the power supply and create fear.

5. Cyberbullying or Cyberstalking

Cyberbullying means insulting, threatening, or troubling someone repeatedly through the internet.

Cyberstalking means continuously following or contacting someone online against their wishes.

Example: Repeatedly sending threatening messages to someone on Instagram or WhatsApp.

6. Cybersquatting

Cybersquatting means registering or using a website domain name related to another company’s brand or trademark to make money unfairly.

The person may try to sell that domain name to the company at a high price.

Example: A person registers a domain name using a famous company’s trademark and demands money from the company to sell it.

7. Creating Malware

Malware means harmful software that can damage computers, steal information, or disturb their normal working.

Criminals create and spread viruses, worms, and spyware to harm computer systems.

Example: Sending a file containing a virus that damages a computer when opened.

8. Data Diddling

Data diddling means changing information stored in a computer system dishonestly to get some benefit.

It may include changing marks, salaries, or bank records.

Example: An employee changes their salary from ₹20,000 to ₹30,000 in the computer records.

9. Denial of Service (DoS) Attack

A DoS attack means sending too many requests to a website or server so that it becomes slow or stops working.

Because of this, normal users cannot access the website.

Example: A criminal sends thousands of requests to a shopping website, causing it to stop working.

10. Data Theft

Data theft means stealing important information from a computer, mobile phone, or online account without permission.

This information may include passwords, bank details, or personal records.

Example: A hacker steals customers’ bank details from a company’s computer system.

11. Doxing

Doxing means sharing someone’s private information on the internet without permission, usually to trouble or threaten them.

This information may include their phone number, home address, or other personal details.

Example: Posting someone’s private phone number and home address on social media to encourage harassment.

12. Human Trafficking

Human trafficking means illegally recruiting or controlling people for exploitation, such as forcing them to work against their wishes.

Criminals may use the internet to find and trick people.

Example: Criminals advertise fake jobs online and then force people to work against their wishes.

13. Espionage

Espionage means secretly collecting private or important information about a person, company, or government.

Criminals may use computers or the internet to steal such information.

Example: A hacker secretly steals a company’s business plans and gives them to another company.

14. Fake Products or Services

This crime means selling fake products or offering false services through the internet to cheat people.

Criminals may create fake shopping websites, antivirus programs, or technical support services.

Example: A fake shopping website collects money for mobile phones but never delivers them.

15. Fraud

Cyber fraud means cheating people using computers or the internet to steal money or get some illegal benefit.

It may include online payment fraud, credit card fraud, or changing bank records.

Example: A criminal uses someone’s stolen credit card details to buy products online.

16. Green Graffiti

Green graffiti means displaying pictures, messages, or advertisements on buildings using lights, lasers, or projectors.

It is not always a cyber crime, but it may be illegal if it breaks local laws or safety rules.

Example: A person projects an advertisement onto a government building without permission, where such activity is prohibited.

17. Harvesting

Harvesting means collecting people’s email addresses, usernames, passwords, or other account details.

It may be illegal when criminals collect this information without permission or use it for fraud.

Example: A criminal creates a fake login page to collect people’s usernames and passwords.

18. Identity Theft

Identity theft means stealing someone’s personal information and using it to pretend to be that person.

Criminals may use stolen details to open accounts, take loans, or commit fraud.

Example: A criminal uses another person’s stolen Aadhaar and PAN details to apply for a loan.

19. Illegal Sales

Illegal sales mean buying or selling prohibited goods or services through the internet.

These may include illegal drugs, weapons, or stolen information.

Example: A criminal sells stolen credit card details through an illegal website.

20. Intellectual Property Theft

Intellectual property theft means stealing another person’s or company’s valuable creations, designs, inventions, or secret business information.

Criminals may use or sell this information without permission.

Example: An employee steals a company’s secret product design and sells it to another company.

21. IPR Violation

IPR stands for Intellectual Property Rights. An IPR violation means using another person’s legally protected work, invention, or brand without permission.

These rights include copyrights, patents, and trademarks.

Example: A company copies another company’s registered logo and uses it on its products without permission.

22. Phishing or Vishing

Phishing means sending fake emails, messages, or website links to trick people into sharing private information.

Vishing means doing the same thing through phone calls. Criminals may ask for passwords, OTPs, or bank details.

Example: A fraudster calls someone pretending to be a bank employee and asks for an OTP to steal money.

23. Scam

A scam means tricking people by making false promises to steal money or personal information.

Scammers may offer fake prizes, jobs, investments, or gifts.

Example: A person receives a message saying they have won ₹10 lakh but must first pay ₹5,000 to receive it.

24. Slander

Slander means making false spoken statements about someone that damage their reputation.

False written statements posted online are generally called libel. Such statements may amount to defamation depending on the law and situation.

Example: A person knowingly spreads false accusations about a teacher through online videos to damage the teacher’s reputation.

25. Software Piracy

Software piracy means copying, sharing, selling, or using paid software illegally without permission or a valid license.

It causes financial loss to software companies.

Example: Downloading a cracked version of paid software and sharing it with friends without permission.

26. Spamming

Spamming means sending unwanted emails or messages to many people.

These messages may contain advertisements, fake offers, or harmful links.

Example: A person sends thousands of unwanted promotional emails to different people.

27. Spoofing

Spoofing means pretending to be someone else by using a fake email address, phone number, or website.

Criminals do this to make people believe that the message or website is real.

Example: A fraudster sends a fake email that looks like it came from a bank.

28. Swatting

Swatting means making a false emergency complaint to send police or emergency services to another person’s location.

It can waste police time and put innocent people in danger.

Example: A person falsely reports a serious crime at someone’s house so that police are sent there.

29. Theft

Computer-related theft means stealing computer equipment, software, or important digital information.

It may involve stealing physical devices or information stored on them.

Example: An employee steals a company laptop containing important business information.

30. Typosquatting

Typosquatting means creating a website with a name that is slightly misspelled compared to a popular website.

Criminals use such websites to trick people who accidentally type the wrong website address.

Example: A criminal creates a fake bank website with one wrong letter in its address to steal login details.

31. Unauthorized Access

Unauthorized access means entering someone’s computer, network, or online account without permission.

Criminals may use this access to see, steal, change, or delete information.

Example: A student enters the college computer system without permission to see private student records.

32. Vandalism

Computer vandalism means intentionally damaging computer equipment, software, websites, or stored information.

Criminals may delete files, damage systems, or change website content.

Example: A hacker enters a school website and deletes important information.

33. Wiretapping

Wiretapping means secretly listening to or recording someone’s phone calls or electronic conversations without legal permission.

Criminals may do this to steal private or important information.

Example: A criminal secretly listens to someone’s phone calls to collect private information.

34. Ransomware

Ransomware is harmful software that locks a computer or encrypts its files and demands money to restore access.

The user may not be able to open important files until they are unlocked or recovered.

Example: A hacker encrypts a hospital’s computer files and demands ₹5 lakh to unlock them.

Conclusion

Cyber crimes are illegal activities carried out using computers, mobile phones, and the internet. They may involve stealing money, personal information, damaging systems, or troubling people online.

We can reduce cyber crimes by using strong passwords, avoiding unknown links, updating software, and protecting our personal information.
