Q4. Do’s and Don’ts of Collecting Digital Evidence

Introduction

Digital evidence means information collected from computers, mobile phones, and other digital devices during a cyber crime investigation.

Investigators must collect and handle this evidence carefully so that it is not lost, damaged, or changed.

A. Do’s of Collecting Digital Evidence

1. Keep Records of All Actions

Investigators should write down everything they do while collecting and checking evidence.

They should record what they did, when they did it, and why they did it.

This helps keep a proper record of the investigation.

2. Follow the Five Rules of Evidence

Investigators should follow five important rules to make evidence useful in court.

  • Admissible: Evidence must be legally accepted in court.
  • Authentic: Evidence must be real and genuine.
  • Complete: Evidence must include all important information.
  • Reliable: Evidence must be collected using correct methods.
  • Believable: Evidence must be clear and convincing.

3. Create an Exact Copy of the Evidence

Investigators should create an exact copy of the original digital evidence, called a forensic image.

They should check this copy instead of working directly on the original evidence.

Investigators can compare hash values to check that the copy matches the original.

This helps keep the original evidence safe from changes or damage.

4. Be Ready to Explain Evidence in Court

Investigators should keep proper records of how they collected and checked the evidence.

They should be able to explain their methods and findings clearly in court.

This helps the court understand the evidence.

5. Collect Evidence Quickly and Carefully

Investigators should collect important evidence as soon as possible because some information can disappear quickly.

They should follow proper steps to avoid losing or changing evidence.

6. Collect Temporary Evidence First

Investigators should collect temporary information, such as RAM data, first when it is safe and necessary.

This is important because RAM data may disappear when the computer is switched off.

B. Don’ts of Collecting Digital Evidence

1. Do Not Change or Damage Original Evidence

Investigators should not delete, change, or damage the original evidence.

Any changes may make the evidence less reliable in court.

2. Do Not Work Without Proper Knowledge

Investigators should not perform tasks they do not understand.

They should follow safety rules and ask experts for help when needed.

3. Do Not Shut Down the Computer Without Checking

Investigators should not switch off a running computer without checking whether important temporary evidence needs to be collected.

Switching off the computer may erase information stored in RAM.

They should follow proper procedures before shutting it down.

4. Do Not Run Unknown Programs

Investigators should not open unknown software or run unnecessary programs on the computer.

These programs may change, delete, or damage important evidence.

Conclusion

Following the do’s and don’ts helps investigators collect and protect digital evidence properly.

It keeps the evidence safe, accurate, and reliable for use in court.
